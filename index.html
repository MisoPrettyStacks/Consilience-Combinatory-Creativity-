the premise

Two ideas, one convergence

This project sits at the intersection of two long-standing ideas — one about how knowledge fits together, one about how newness gets made.

E.O. Wilson, 1998

Consilience

The unity of knowledge — the idea that insight from biology, physics, art, economics, and every other discipline can be woven into one coherent understanding, instead of staying siloed behind departmental walls.

Margaret Boden

Combinatory Creativity

Novelty born from recombining familiar ideas in ways that produce genuinely emergent value — not a mashup, but a synthesis where the result does something neither original idea could do alone.

from the AI collaborator

Open suggestions

Directions worth exploring, offered by the AI working alongside this project — not settled conclusions, starting points for discussion.

substrate

Cross-domain knowledge graph

Typed edges (causal, structural, functional, analogical) across fields, so agents can traverse from "immune signaling" to "distributed consensus" via an explicit, defensible bridge.

roles

Separate cognitive roles

Domain specialists, a bridge/analogist agent, a critic scoring novelty–surprise–value, and a feasibility agent pulling ideas back toward buildability.

process

Explicit divergence → convergence cycling

Mirror Boden's three tiers directly: recombine, then explore variations, then deliberately try breaking the space's own rules.

evaluation

Population-based selection

Treat ideation as evolutionary search — score, keep, mutate, and cull candidates over time instead of one-shot generation.

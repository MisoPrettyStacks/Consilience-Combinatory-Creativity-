## Why "all rights reserved" instead of an open-source license

Open-source licenses (MIT, Apache 2.0, CC-BY, etc.) are *permissive by
design* — they exist specifically to let anyone reuse the work with few or
no restrictions. That's the opposite of what's wanted here while the
project is being developed and its original ideas are still being
established. "All rights reserved" is the strongest and simplest default
protection under copyright law without requiring any registration.

If a more nuanced structure becomes useful later — for example, opening the
*code* under a share-alike license (like PolyForm Noncommercial or
CC BY-NC-ND) while keeping the *research framing and written IP* fully
reserved — that can be added as the project matures. Note this document is
provided for clarity of intent, not as formal legal advice; consult an
attorney for anything with real commercial or legal stakes.
